Managed Data-as-a-Service Architecture

The decision framework for delivering data to customers: from Reader Accounts and Secure Shares through Native Apps, open Iceberg access, and full SaaS applications -- with the right model for every customer profile.

How Companies Accomplish This Today

  • Direct database logins: Create a user in your data platform for each customer. They connect directly, run queries, and see the raw platform UI. Easy to set up, but difficult to govern -- platform UI changes hit your customers without warning, and you become a support proxy for a product you don't own. On Snowflake, this pattern is not supported by the Terms of Service.
  • Separate platform accounts per customer: Full isolation but extreme operational overhead. Every schema change, every software update must be rolled out N times.
  • Custom portal with JDBC backend: Build a web app that queries the data platform on behalf of customers. Full control but significant engineering investment.
  • File-based delivery: Export data to S3/SFTP for customer pickup. No interactivity, always stale, no governance after delivery.

Roles and People Involved

Product / GTM

Decides the delivery model: what level of self-service, interactivity, and customization customers get.

Legal / Compliance

Ensures the delivery model complies with Snowflake TOS (service bureau restrictions), data processing agreements, and customer contracts.

Platform Engineering

Builds and maintains the delivery infrastructure: shares, apps, APIs, and customer onboarding automation.

Customer Success

Handles the support burden when customers hit issues -- the primary symptom of the direct-login anti-pattern.

Challenges Today

  • UI dependency: Customers using the platform's native UI call your support team whenever the vendor ships a UI change. You're on the hook for a product you don't control.
  • TOS violations: Providing Snowflake access to third parties as a service bureau is prohibited under standard TOS without an MSP addendum. Many companies don't know this until it's flagged.
  • No feature gating: Direct logins give all customers the same capabilities. No way to differentiate tiers, restrict features, or control the experience.
  • Customer tooling diversity: Each customer brings their own BI tools, scripts, and workflows to your platform. You can't standardize the experience or ensure quality.
  • Customers on other engines: Customers on Spark, Trino, or DuckDB are left out entirely unless you export to files or stand up a separate access layer.

The Anti-Pattern: Giving Customers Direct Account Access

This pattern violates Snowflake's Terms of Service -- Snowflake does not condone it

The pattern is straightforward: a provider creates Snowflake users in their own account for each customer, gives them a role scoped to their data, and lets them log in to Snowsight or connect via JDBC/ODBC. It may appear easy to set up, but it violates Snowflake's Terms of Service and creates serious technical, security, and operational problems that compound over time.

How it typically looks
-- The anti-pattern: creating Snowflake users for your customers
CREATE USER acme_analyst
  PASSWORD      = '...'
  DEFAULT_ROLE  = acme_role
  DEFAULT_WAREHOUSE = customer_wh;

CREATE ROLE acme_role;
GRANT USAGE ON WAREHOUSE customer_wh TO ROLE acme_role;
GRANT USAGE ON DATABASE product_db TO ROLE acme_role;
GRANT SELECT ON TABLE product_db.acme.reports TO ROLE acme_role;

-- Customer logs in to Snowsight at https://<your-account>.snowflakecomputing.com
-- They see YOUR account, YOUR warehouses, YOUR Snowsight UI
Terms of Service Violation
  • Section 2.3 of the TOS prohibits making the Snowflake Technology available to a third party "or in a service bureau or outsourcing offering"
  • The TOS carves out an exception for "Service features expressly intended to enable Customer to provide its third parties with access to Customer Data" -- i.e., Reader Accounts, Secure Sharing, Listings, and Native Apps
  • Creating Snowflake users for your customers in your account does not fall under this exception -- it is granting direct platform access, not using an approved sharing feature
  • An MSP addendum exists for managed service providers (negotiated through your Snowflake account team), but it has specific requirements and obligations
  • Enforcement can range from a warning to account suspension -- risk increases with scale
Shared Security Model Breakdown
  • Customer users exist in your identity plane -- a misconfigured role can expose other tenants' data
  • Customers can see your account structure: warehouse names, database names, and metadata via SHOW commands
  • You cannot enforce your customer's security policies (SSO, MFA) -- they use your account's auth settings
  • ACCOUNTADMIN remains a single point of compromise for all customer data
Operational Risks
  • Customer queries compete for warehouse resources -- one customer's heavy query affects all others
  • Snowsight UI changes hit your customers without notice -- you become Snowflake's support proxy
  • No feature gating: every customer gets the same Snowflake capabilities regardless of their plan tier
  • Cost attribution is approximate at best -- query_tag helps but requires consistent discipline
Scaling Problems
  • User management grows linearly: onboarding, offboarding, password resets, and role changes for every customer user
  • Customer-specific customization is impossible -- everyone sees the same Snowsight interface
  • Audit and compliance become complex: separating customer activity in ACCESS_HISTORY requires careful tagging
  • Migrating away later is painful -- customers have built workflows against your account's URLs and object names

The fix: Every risk above is addressed by one of the 8 delivery models below. Reader Accounts give non-Snowflake customers SQL access without TOS issues. Secure Shares give Snowflake customers zero-copy data in their own account. Native Apps add code, UI, and agents on top. The right model eliminates the anti-pattern entirely.

What Snowflake Offers

Snowflake provides a spectrum of data delivery models -- from zero-code shares to full application frameworks. The right choice depends on who your customer is, what tools they use, and how much control you need.

  • For customers without Snowflake: Reader Accounts (SQL access, you pay compute), Open Sharing via Iceberg (any engine reads your data via standard REST catalog), or a Custom SaaS App (full control).
  • For customers with Snowflake: Secure Data Sharing (zero-copy, always live), Declarative Native Apps (data + code, no setup script), Full Native Apps (complete application framework), MCP (AI tool integration), or Data Clean Rooms (privacy-safe analytics).
  • Cross-cutting: Cortex Agents can be layered onto any model that involves Snowflake, adding natural-language querying to shared data or apps.

Delivery Models

Customer Does NOT Have Snowflake
1

Reader Accounts

Managed Snowflake accounts for customers without their own. Provider pays compute. SQL + Snowsight access.

GA You Pay
▾
Key Capabilities
  • Customers get a full Snowflake login (Snowsight, SQL worksheets)
  • Provider creates and manages the account entirely
  • Can only consume data shared from the provider account
  • Customers can create materialized views on shared data
Constraints
  • Max 20 reader accounts per provider (contact support to increase)
  • Provider pays for all compute credits (unlimited unless resource monitor set)
  • Readers cannot upload data, create pipes, stages, or shares
  • 7-day retention period after dropping an account before slot is freed
  • Same edition and region as the provider account
-- Create a reader account for a customer
USE ROLE ACCOUNTADMIN;
CREATE MANAGED ACCOUNT acme_reader
  ADMIN_NAME     = 'acme_admin',
  ADMIN_PASSWORD = '<strong-password>',
  TYPE           = READER;

-- Create a share and grant it to the reader
CREATE SHARE acme_share;
GRANT USAGE ON DATABASE product_db TO SHARE acme_share;
GRANT USAGE ON SCHEMA product_db.serving TO SHARE acme_share;
GRANT SELECT ON TABLE product_db.serving.reports TO SHARE acme_share;

-- Assign the share to the reader account
ALTER SHARE acme_share ADD ACCOUNT = <reader_account_locator>;
2

Open Sharing (Iceberg)

Expose Snowflake-managed Iceberg tables via REST catalog. Customers read with Spark, Trino, DuckDB, Flink -- any Iceberg-compatible engine.

Preview Open Format
▾

Two paths exist for Iceberg-based open sharing. Horizon Catalog (GA) exposes a Polaris-compatible REST endpoint from your Snowflake account -- external engines connect directly. Open Data Sharing (Public Preview) adds a formal sharing layer with external consumer identities and PAT-based auth for non-Snowflake customers.

Horizon Catalog (GA)
  • Built into every Snowflake account -- no separate product
  • Polaris-compatible Apache Iceberg REST endpoint at https://<org>-<acct>.snowflakecomputing.com/polaris/api/catalog
  • Supports: Spark, Flink, Trino, DuckDB, Dremio, StarRocks, PyIceberg, Doris
  • Auth: External OAuth, key-pair (JWT), PAT, Workload Identity Federation
  • Read and write both GA. 0.5 credits / million API calls (billing starts H2 2026)
Open Data Sharing (Public Preview)
  • CREATE EXTERNAL CONSUMER for non-Snowflake customers
  • Issue a PAT per consumer for authenticated catalog access
  • CREATE EXTERNAL LISTING with share + open_sharing config
  • Same-region only during Preview (no cross-region)
  • Only PAT auth during Preview; OAuth planned
-- Path A: Horizon Catalog -- external engine reads your Iceberg table
-- (Spark config pointing at your Snowflake account's REST endpoint)
-- catalog_uri = https://<org>-<acct>.snowflakecomputing.com/polaris/api/catalog
-- warehouse  = <database_name>

-- Path B: Open Data Sharing -- formal sharing with external consumers
USE ROLE ACCOUNTADMIN;

-- 1. Create the Iceberg table
CREATE ICEBERG TABLE product_db.serving.reports (
  tenant_id  VARCHAR,
  report_id  INT,
  report_data VARIANT
) EXTERNAL_VOLUME = 'SNOWFLAKE_MANAGED'
  CATALOG = 'SNOWFLAKE';

-- 2. Create an external consumer identity
CREATE EXTERNAL CONSUMER acme_ext;

-- 3. Issue a PAT (save the secret -- it can't be retrieved later)
ALTER EXTERNAL CONSUMER acme_ext ADD PAT acme_pat;

-- 4. Create share and grant access to the Iceberg table
CREATE SHARE acme_open_share;
GRANT USAGE ON DATABASE product_db TO SHARE acme_open_share;
GRANT USAGE ON SCHEMA product_db.serving TO SHARE acme_open_share;
GRANT SELECT ON TABLE product_db.serving.reports TO SHARE acme_open_share;

-- 5. Create external listing
CREATE EXTERNAL LISTING acme_listing SHARE acme_open_share AS
$$
title: "Product Reports - Acme"
description: "Live Iceberg data for external consumption"
open_sharing:
  catalog_identifier: "acme-reports-catalog"
listing_terms:
  type: "OFFLINE"
external_targets:
  access:
    - external_consumers: [ACME_EXT]
$$;

-- 6. Get the catalog URL for the customer
CALL SYSTEM$GET_LISTING_URL_FOR_EXTERNAL_CONSUMER('ACME_LISTING');

Custom SaaS Application

Your own web application (App Runtime, SPCS, or external hosting). Full branding, feature gating, user management. Highest investment, maximum control.

GA High Effort
▾
Key Capabilities
  • Full control over UI, branding, user experience, and feature gating
  • Custom user management and authentication (not Snowflake users)
  • Tier-based feature access (free/pro/enterprise)
  • Can embed Cortex Agents for AI-powered features
  • Snowflake App Runtime (Next.js on SPCS) or external hosting
When to Choose This
  • Customer shouldn't know or care that Snowflake is involved
  • You need white-label delivery or complex multi-tenant UX
  • Feature gating, usage limits, and self-service are required
  • You already have a web app and Snowflake is the backend
Trade-offs
  • Significant engineering investment (frontend, auth, infra)
  • You own the support and operations burden
  • Not distributable via Snowflake Marketplace
Customer HAS Snowflake
4

Secure Data Sharing

Zero-copy sharing of live data to customer Snowflake accounts. No ETL, always current, revocable instantly. Direct shares or listings (free / paid / trial).

GA Zero-Copy
▾
Listing Types
  • Free: Unlimited access, auto-fulfillment or manual replication for cross-region
  • Paid: Consumer pays. Cross-region requires auto-fulfillment
  • Limited Trial: Free sample of a paid product, or time-limited full access
  • Personalized: Private listing to specific accounts. Cannot use auto-fulfillment
Cross-Region (Auto-Fulfillment)
  • Automatically replicates data to consumer's region on demand
  • Paid listings must use auto-fulfillment for cross-region
  • Provider cost incurred only when consumer demand exists
  • Shareable objects: tables, views, secure views, secure UDFs, Iceberg tables
-- Create a share with governed data access
CREATE SHARE analytics_share;
GRANT USAGE ON DATABASE product_db TO SHARE analytics_share;
GRANT USAGE ON SCHEMA product_db.serving TO SHARE analytics_share;

-- Share a secure view (consumers see only their data)
GRANT SELECT ON VIEW product_db.serving.tenant_reports_vw
  TO SHARE analytics_share;

-- Add a consumer account (same region = instant)
ALTER SHARE analytics_share ADD ACCOUNT = <consumer_account>;

-- Or publish as a Marketplace listing for broader distribution
-- (listings support free, paid, trial, and personalized types)
5

Declarative Native App (TYPE=DATA)

Data + notebooks + semantic views + agents packaged as an installable app. No setup script -- everything declared in a YAML manifest.

GA No Setup Script
▾
Included Objects
  • Data: Tables (standard, dynamic, Iceberg), views
  • Semantic views: Referenced tables must also be shared
  • Functions and procedures: UDFs, stored procedures (shared by copy)
  • Cortex Agents: Bundle AI assistants with the data
  • Workspaces: Notebooks, docs, images, sample data
Constraints
  • Data objects and logic objects must be in different schemas
  • Data shared by reference, code shared by copy
  • Dynamic tables and Iceberg tables are read-only and don't auto-refresh in remote regions
  • Versioned via RELEASE LIVE VERSION (no setup script versioning)
-- Create a declarative native app package
CREATE APPLICATION PACKAGE analytics_pkg TYPE = DATA;

-- manifest.yml (placed at package root)
-- roles:
--   - VIEWER:
--       comment: "View dashboards and summaries"
--   - ANALYST:
--       comment: "Query detailed tables and semantic views"
-- shared_content:
--   databases:
--     - ANALYTICS_DB:
--         schemas:
--           - DATA_SCHEMA:            # data objects
--               tables:
--                 - USAGE_METRICS:  { roles: [ANALYST] }
--               semantic_views:
--                 - USAGE_SV:       { roles: [ANALYST] }
--           - LOGIC_SCHEMA:           # code objects (separate schema!)
--               procedures:
--                 - RUN_REPORT():   { roles: [ANALYST] }
--               cortex_agents:
--                 - USAGE_AGENT:    { roles: [VIEWER] }
-- application_content:
--   workspaces:
--     - GETTING_STARTED:
--         source: notebooks/
--         roles: [VIEWER, ANALYST]

-- Release to consumers
ALTER APPLICATION PACKAGE analytics_pkg RELEASE LIVE VERSION;

Full Native App (with setup script)

Data + code + Streamlit UI + agents + optional containers (SPCS). Full provider control, versioned, Marketplace-distributable.

GA Med-High Effort
▾
What's Included
  • Setup script (SQL): Runs on install/upgrade, creates objects, roles, grants
  • Streamlit UI: Default app dashboard via default_streamlit_app
  • Stored procedures and UDFs: Python/Java via Snowpark
  • Container services (SPCS): GPU-enabled, Docker images (AWS/Azure)
  • Cortex Agents: Embedded AI assistants
  • Versioning: Versions + patches + release channels + release directives
Declarative vs Full Native App
  • Declarative (TYPE=DATA): No setup script, YAML-only, lighter. Best when sharing data + notebooks + semantic views
  • Full: Setup script required, more powerful. Use when you need Streamlit UI, complex logic, SPCS containers, or consumer-account objects
  • Both support Cortex Agents, UDFs, procedures
  • Both are Marketplace-distributable
-- Full Native App manifest (manifest_version: 2)
-- artifacts:
--   setup_script: scripts/setup.sql
--   readme: docs/README.md
--   default_streamlit_app: apps/main.py
-- privileges:
--   - CREATE TABLE: { description: "Store app state" }
-- references:
--   - customer_data:
--       label: "Customer Table"
--       privileges: [SELECT]
--       object_type: Table
--       register_callback: app.update_reference

-- Create package and add a version
CREATE APPLICATION PACKAGE my_analytics_app;
ALTER APPLICATION PACKAGE my_analytics_app
  ADD VERSION v1 USING '@my_stage/v1';

-- Install and test locally
CREATE APPLICATION my_app
  FROM APPLICATION PACKAGE my_analytics_app
  USING VERSION v1;

MCP Connection

Expose data to AI tools and BI platforms via Model Context Protocol. Snowflake-managed MCP server with Cortex Search, Analyst, and custom tools.

GA AI Tools
▾
Key Capabilities
  • Snowflake-managed, fully-hosted MCP server -- a SQL object
  • Tool types: CORTEX_SEARCH_SERVICE_QUERY, CORTEX_ANALYST_MESSAGE, Agents, SQL execution, custom tools
  • Clients: Cursor, Claude Desktop, Cortex Code, any MCP-compatible client

When to Choose This

  • You want schema-aware, authenticated data access for AI agents
  • Low engineering effort -- define tools in a YAML spec
  • Not available in government regions

Data Clean Room

Privacy-safe analytics where customers run approved query templates but never see raw data. Cross-cloud supported. Covered in detail on page 6.3.

GA Privacy
▾
Key Capabilities

  • Pre-built templates: audience overlap, activation, attribution
  • Custom SQL templates with obfuscation (hide your query IP)

When to Choose This

  • Obfuscated templates require consumer Enterprise edition

A progressive walkthrough: start with the simplest model and build up to the most complete. Each step shows a different delivery model applied to the same dataset.

1

The Data Product

You're a SaaS analytics company. Your data product is tenant-specific usage reports: API calls, feature adoption, cost breakdowns. The same dataset will be delivered via different models to different customer segments.

This is the starting point -- a table in your Snowflake account that every customer needs access to, filtered to their data.

-- The data product: per-tenant analytics reports
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE product_db.serving.tenant_reports (
  tenant_id    VARCHAR NOT NULL,
  report_date  DATE NOT NULL,
  metric_name  VARCHAR NOT NULL,
  metric_value NUMBER(18,4),
  dimension    VARCHAR,
  detail       VARIANT
);

-- Secure view: each consumer only sees their data
CREATE OR REPLACE SECURE VIEW
  product_db.serving.tenant_reports_vw
AS
SELECT * FROM product_db.serving.tenant_reports
WHERE tenant_id = CURRENT_ACCOUNT();
-- For shares: CURRENT_ACCOUNT() resolves to consumer

Path A: Reader Account

SMB customer "Acme" doesn't have Snowflake. Create a reader account, share the secure view, and they get SQL + Snowsight access. You pay for their compute.

Trade-off: Fastest to set up, but you absorb compute costs and they see the Snowsight UI (which you don't control).

-- Create reader account for Acme

  ADMIN_PASSWORD = '<strong-password>',

-- Share the secure view
CREATE SHARE acme_share;
GRANT USAGE ON DATABASE product_db TO SHARE acme_share;
GRANT USAGE ON SCHEMA product_db.serving
  TO SHARE acme_share;
GRANT SELECT ON VIEW
  product_db.serving.tenant_reports_vw
  TO SHARE acme_share;

-- Add the reader account to the share
ALTER SHARE acme_share

-- Protect yourself: set a resource monitor
-- (provider pays compute for reader accounts)

Path B: Secure Data Sharing

Enterprise customer "Globex" has their own Snowflake account. Share the same secure view -- zero-copy, always live. Globex pays their own compute. Publish as a free listing if they're in a different region.

Trade-off: No compute cost for you, always up to date, but customer sees raw data in their own tools.

Decision: Which Model for Which Customer?

The same data product, four delivery paths. The choice depends on the customer profile, not the data. Many SaaS companies use 2-3 models simultaneously for different customer segments.

CustomerHas Snowflake?NeedsModel
Acme (SMB)NoSQL accessReader Account
Globex (Enterprise)YesLive data in their toolsSecure Share
Initech (Mid-market)YesData + notebooks + AIDeclarative App
DataCorp (Spark shop)NoIceberg in their lakehouseOpen Sharing

Key insight: Start with the simplest model that meets the customer's needs. You can always upgrade a Reader Account customer to a Native App later without changing the underlying data.

6

Add an AI Layer (Any Model)

Cortex Agents can be layered onto models 4-6 (any model where the consumer has Snowflake). Create a semantic view over the shared data, create an agent, and bundle it -- customers get natural language access to their data.

See 4.1 Embedded Agents for the full walkthrough of CREATE AGENT syntax and multi-tenant agent variables.

-- Semantic view for natural language querying
CREATE OR REPLACE SEMANTIC VIEW
  product_db.analytics.reports_sv
TABLES (
  reports_t AS product_db.serving.tenant_reports
    PRIMARY KEY (tenant_id, report_date, metric_name)
    COMMENT = 'Per-tenant analytics reports'
)
DIMENSIONS (
  reports_t.tenant   AS reports_t.tenant_id
    COMMENT = 'Customer organization',
  reports_t.date     AS reports_t.report_date
    COMMENT = 'Report date',
  reports_t.metric   AS reports_t.metric_name
    COMMENT = 'Metric name'
)
METRICS (
  reports_t.value    AS
    SUM(reports_t.metric_value)
    COMMENT = 'Total metric value'
);

-- Agent (can be bundled in a Declarative or Full Native App)
CREATE OR REPLACE AGENT product_db.agents.report_agent
FROM SPECIFICATION $$
models:
  base_model: claude-3.5-sonnet
orchestration: cortex_default
instructions: "You answer questions about tenant analytics."

Multi-Model Architecture

The final state: one data product, multiple distribution models, each optimized for the customer segment. Automation provisions new customers into the right model based on their profile.

Monitor via LISTING_ACCESS_HISTORY (who queries shared data), LISTING_REFRESH_HISTORY (cross-region sync status), and your application's metering pipeline (see 1.3 Usage Metering).
