Usage Metering and Billing

Building consumption-based billing on Snowflake: tracking every tenant's usage in real time, choosing the right pricing model, and translating metered events into customer invoices, all without a custom metering pipeline.

How Companies Accomplish This Today

  • Application-level counters: Increment Redis/DynamoDB counters for every API call, feature usage, or record created. Requires instrumentation in every service. Miss one, and you're giving away a feature for free.
  • Log aggregation pipelines: Ship application logs to Kafka, aggregate in Flink or Spark, write to a billing DB. Complex, expensive, and typically 1-6 hours behind real time.
  • Third-party metering platforms: Tools like Metronome, Amberflo, or Orb that sit between your app and billing system. Add cost, another integration point, and a data residency concern.
  • Manual reconciliation: Finance teams manually reconcile infrastructure costs with customer accounts monthly. Doesn't scale past ~100 customers.

Roles and People Involved

Revenue Operations

Translates usage data into invoices, manages billing disputes, and ensures metering accuracy matches contractual terms.

Product / Pricing

Designs usage-based pricing models, defines billable units, and sets tier thresholds. Owns the value metric decision.

Backend Engineering

Instruments the application to emit metering events and integrates the billing pipeline with Stripe, Chargebee, or a custom system.

Customer Success

Helps customers understand their usage, provides cost optimization guidance, and handles bill-shock escalations.

Challenges Today

  • Instrumentation debt: Every new feature needs metering code. Miss one endpoint and you're giving away value for free.
  • Accuracy at scale: At millions of events per day, even small counting errors compound into significant billing discrepancies.
  • Real-time vs. batch: Customers want to see their current usage in real time, but metering pipelines are typically batch-processed with hours of lag.
  • Billing flexibility: Changing pricing models (per-seat to per-query to per-AI-call) requires re-engineering the entire metering pipeline.
  • Bill shock: 78% of IT leaders reported unexpected charges from consumption-based SaaS in 2026 (Zylo). Without usage alerts and estimators, consumption pricing erodes trust.

What Snowflake Offers

Snowflake gives you the metering infrastructure as a byproduct of running your platform. Every query, every AI call, every byte stored is already tracked in ACCOUNT_USAGE views. Layer application-level events on top, and you have a complete billing pipeline in SQL.

  • Platform metering for free: QUERY_ATTRIBUTION_HISTORY, CORTEX_AI_FUNCTIONS_USAGE_HISTORY, and TABLE_STORAGE_METRICS already track credits and bytes per tenant (via query_tag). No additional instrumentation for infrastructure costs.
  • Application event metering: Stream custom events (API calls, feature invocations) via Snowpipe Streaming. Sub-second latency from your app to the metering table.
  • Real-time aggregation: Dynamic tables continuously compute per-tenant billing totals. TARGET_LAG = 1 minute means your customer's usage dashboard is always current.
  • Flexible rating logic: Change your billing unit from "queries" to "report views" to "AI tokens" with a SQL change. No pipeline rebuild.
  • Auditable by default: Every metering record is immutable, timestamped, and queryable. Dispute resolution is a Time Travel query, not a forensic investigation.

The Metering Stack on Snowflake

Consumption billing has three stages. Snowflake handles all three with native capabilities:

Stage 1

Meter

Capture raw usage events: API calls from your app (Snowpipe Streaming), platform costs from ACCOUNT_USAGE views, AI credits from CORTEX_USAGE. Every event has tenant_id + timestamp.

Stage 2

Rate

Convert raw events into billable units. A Dynamic Table applies your pricing logic: map "5 queries" to "1 report view", apply tier discounts, deduct committed use. Updated continuously.

Stage 3

Invoice

Snapshot the rated usage at period end. Apply final pricing rules, generate a JSON invoice record, and push to Stripe/Chargebee via external access integration.

Choosing Your Pricing Model

77% of large SaaS companies now use some form of consumption pricing (Metronome 2025). In 2026, hybrid (platform fee + usage) is the dominant pattern at 37% adoption. For a full comparison of pricing model options (per-seat, pure usage, hybrid, committed consumption, and credit-based), see the Section 1 Capstone Playbook, which covers the strategic decision framework, value metric design, and rollout phases.

This page assumes you've chosen a consumption or hybrid model. The metering pipeline below works for any of them; the only thing that changes is your pricing_rules table in Step 5.

From COGS to Price: Setting Your Rates

Section 1.2 Unit Cost Attribution gives you the cost floor: what it costs you to serve each tenant. This section shows how to set your customer-facing price on top of that.

Step 1
Know Your Cost Floor

Query QUERY_ATTRIBUTION_HISTORY and CORTEX_AI_FUNCTIONS_USAGE_HISTORY to calculate your blended cost per billable unit. Example: if one "report view" costs you 0.003 credits ($0.009), that's your floor.

Step 2
Apply Target Margin

Target 70-80% gross margin. If your cost floor is $0.009, your customer price is $0.03-$0.045 per unit. Build in headroom for growth, support, and R&D amortization.

Step 3
Prevent Bill Shock

Offer committed use agreements (lower per-unit rates for volume commitments). Add usage alerts at 50%/80%/100% of committed tier. Show a real-time cost estimator in your product.

Key insight: Choose a "value metric" that correlates with customer value, not just your cost. "Report views" is better than "queries" because the customer understands the value of a report. "AI insights generated" is better than "tokens consumed" because the customer pays for the outcome, not the input.

Helping Tenants Control Their Own Spend

In a managed SaaS model, you (the provider) own the Snowflake account. Your tenants can't log in to Snowsight and set their own budgets. But you can build self-service spend controls into your product using Snowflake's governance features as the enforcement layer.

Resource Monitors
GA · Hard Stop

Attach a credit quota to a tenant's warehouse. When the quota hits, the warehouse suspends. Hard stop, no overage. Your app can adjust the limit on the tenant's behalf for self-service. Covers warehouse compute only. No serverless, AI, or storage.

Show SQL 
-- Create a 100-credit monthly monitor for Acme's warehouse
CREATE RESOURCE MONITOR acme_monitor
  WITH CREDIT_QUOTA = 100
  FREQUENCY = MONTHLY
  START_TIMESTAMP = IMMEDIATELY
  TRIGGERS
    ON 75 PERCENT DO NOTIFY
    ON 90 PERCENT DO NOTIFY
    ON 100 PERCENT DO SUSPEND;

ALTER WAREHOUSE acme_wh
  SET RESOURCE_MONITOR = acme_monitor;

Custom Budgets
GA · Alert + Action

Tag tenant objects by cost center and create a budget that tracks all tagged resources. Monitors all services: compute, serverless, AI, clustering, and agents. Alert only (no hard stop), but can trigger automated actions at threshold. Your app can adjust the spending limit on the tenant's behalf for self-service. Max 100 budgets per account.

Show SQL 
-- Tag tenant objects for budget tracking
ALTER WAREHOUSE acme_wh
  SET TAG cost_center = 'acme';
ALTER SCHEMA app_db.acme_data
  SET TAG cost_center = 'acme';

-- Create a custom budget object
CREATE SNOWFLAKE.CORE.BUDGET acme_budget();

-- Set credit spending limit (credits/month)
CALL acme_budget!SET_SPENDING_LIMIT(500);

-- Link the budget to the tag (tracks all tagged objects)
CALL acme_budget!SET_RESOURCE_TAGS(
  [[SYSTEM$REFERENCE('TAG',
    'cost_mgmt.tags.cost_center',
    'SESSION', 'APPLYBUDGET'),
  'acme']]
);

Per-User Quotas
Public Preview · Hard Stop

Daily or monthly credit caps per user. When hit, AI function calls and agent interactions pause. Map each tenant to a service user and set their quota. Best for AI spend control; pairs with function-level access controls.

Show SQL 
-- Create a per-user quota object
CREATE SNOWFLAKE.CORE.QUOTA acme_ai_quota();

-- Scope to AI services
CALL acme_ai_quota!ADD_SHARED_RESOURCE('CORTEX AGENT');
CALL acme_ai_quota!ADD_SHARED_RESOURCE('AI FUNCTION');

-- Set per-user monthly and daily limits
CALL acme_ai_quota!SET_PER_USER_LIMIT(1000);          -- monthly
CALL acme_ai_quota!SET_PER_USER_LIMIT(50, 'DAILY');   -- daily

-- Enable hard stop when limit is reached
CALL acme_ai_quota!SET_BLOCK_ENFORCEMENT_ENABLED(
  TRUE, TRUE);

Application-Level Controls
DIY · Full Flexibility

Check the tenant's current spend against their plan limit before allowing expensive operations. Full flexibility: alerts, auto-downgrade, buy more. No platform dependency: your logic, your limits, your UX.

Show SQL 
-- Check tenant spend before allowing an expensive operation
SELECT SUM(line_amount) AS current_spend
FROM billing.rated_usage
WHERE tenant_id = 'acme'
  AND billing_month = DATE_TRUNC('month',
        CURRENT_DATE());

Snowflake Features Used

QUERY_ATTRIBUTION_HISTORY

Per-query credit attribution on standard warehouses. Includes query_tag for tenant-level cost breakdowns. The foundation for compute-based COGS.

TABLE_STORAGE_METRICS

Per-table storage bytes (active, Time Travel, Failsafe). For OPT models, gives per-tenant storage directly. For MTT, estimate via row counts.

Dynamic Tables

Continuously materialized billing aggregations. Running totals per tenant per billing period, updated automatically. TARGET_LAG = 1 minute for real-time dashboards.

Snowpipe Streaming

Ingest application-level usage events in real time. Sub-second latency from your app's API middleware to the metering table.

Event Tables

Capture structured telemetry from UDFs, procedures, and SPCS services. Track function invocations, errors, and latency per tenant automatically.

Time Travel

Query metering data as it existed at any point in the past. Dispute resolution is a SQL query: "show me what the meter read at invoice time."

Define the Metering Events Table

A single table captures every billable event: API calls from your app, compute from Snowflake, AI invocations, storage snapshots. Every row has tenant_id, event_type, quantity, and optional metadata.

This is the "raw meter," the single source of truth for all billing.

CREATE TABLE billing.metering_events (
  event_id     VARCHAR DEFAULT UUID_STRING(),
  tenant_id    VARCHAR NOT NULL,
  event_type   VARCHAR NOT NULL,
  metric_name  VARCHAR NOT NULL,
  quantity     NUMBER(18,4) NOT NULL,
  metadata     VARIANT,
  event_ts     TIMESTAMP_NTZ DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP()
);

Stream Events from Your App

Your API middleware intercepts every tenant request and emits a metering event via Snowpipe Streaming. No batch jobs, no separate metering service. Events land in Snowflake in under a second.

This replaces the Kafka → Flink → Redis → billing DB pipeline with a single SDK call.

# FastAPI middleware that emits metering events
from snowflake.streaming import SnowflakeStreamingClient

client = SnowflakeStreamingClient(...)
channel = client.open_channel(
    name="metering_channel",
    database="APP_DB",
    schema="BILLING",
    table="METERING_EVENTS"
)

async def metering_middleware(request, call_next):
    # Get tenant from auth context
    tenant_id = request.state.tenant_id
    response = await call_next(request)

    # Emit metering event -- sub-second to Snowflake
    channel.insert_row({
        "tenant_id":   tenant_id,
        "event_type":  "app",
        "metric_name": "api_calls",
        "quantity":    1,
        "metadata":    {
            "endpoint": request.url.path,
            "method":   request.method,
            "status":   response.status_code
        }
    })
    return response

Aggregate into Billing Periods

A dynamic table continuously computes per-tenant, per-month totals by metric. With TARGET_LAG = '1 minute', your customer's usage dashboard is always within 60 seconds of current.

No cron jobs, no schedulers, no ETL orchestration. The aggregation just runs.

CREATE OR REPLACE DYNAMIC TABLE billing.usage_summary
  TARGET_LAG = '1 minute'
  WAREHOUSE = billing_wh
AS
SELECT
  tenant_id,
  DATE_TRUNC('month', event_ts) AS billing_month,
  metric_name,
  SUM(quantity)              AS total_quantity,
  COUNT(*)                   AS event_count,
  MIN(event_ts)              AS first_event,
  MAX(event_ts)              AS last_event
FROM billing.metering_events
GROUP BY tenant_id, billing_month, metric_name;

Enrich with Platform Costs

Join your application events with Snowflake's ACCOUNT_USAGE views to add actual infrastructure costs. This is the bridge to 1.2 Unit Cost Attribution.

Now you have both sides: what the customer consumed (your value metric) and what it cost you (your COGS). The ratio is your gross margin.

-- Compute costs per tenant from QUERY_ATTRIBUTION
INSERT INTO billing.metering_events
  (tenant_id, event_type, metric_name, quantity)
SELECT
  PARSE_JSON(query_tag):'tenant_id'::VARCHAR,
  'compute',
  'credits_used',
  SUM(credits_used_compute)
FROM snowflake.account_usage.query_attribution_history
WHERE start_time >= DATE_TRUNC('day', CURRENT_DATE())
  AND query_tag IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY 1;

-- AI costs per tenant from CORTEX_USAGE
INSERT INTO billing.metering_events
  (tenant_id, event_type, metric_name, quantity)
SELECT
  PARSE_JSON(query_tag):'tenant_id'::VARCHAR,
  'ai',
  'ai_credits',
  SUM(credits_used)
FROM snowflake.account_usage
  .cortex_ai_functions_usage_history
WHERE start_time >= DATE_TRUNC('day', CURRENT_DATE())
  AND query_tag IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY 1;

Apply Rating Logic

Convert raw metered quantities into dollar amounts using your pricing rules. This is where you define what a "unit" costs the customer, and where committed use discounts and tier pricing apply.

The pricing table is a regular Snowflake table. Change a rate, and the next billing cycle reflects it. No code deploy.

-- Pricing rules table (maintained by RevOps)
CREATE TABLE billing.pricing_rules (
  metric_name    VARCHAR,
  tier_min       NUMBER,
  tier_max       NUMBER,
  price_per_unit NUMBER(10,6),
  description    VARCHAR
);

INSERT INTO billing.pricing_rules VALUES
  ('report_views',     0,  1000, 0.050, 'First 1K views'),
  ('report_views',  1001,  5000, 0.035, '1K-5K tier'),
  ('report_views',  5001, 99999, 0.020, 'Volume tier'),
  ('api_calls',        0, 99999, 0.001, 'Per API call'),
  ('ai_credits',       0, 99999, 3.000, 'Per AI credit'),
  ('gb_stored',        0, 99999, 0.400, 'Per GB/month');

-- Rated usage: apply tiered pricing
CREATE OR REPLACE DYNAMIC TABLE billing.rated_usage
  TARGET_LAG = '5 minutes'
  WAREHOUSE = billing_wh
AS
SELECT
  u.tenant_id,
  u.billing_month,
  u.metric_name,
  u.total_quantity,
  p.price_per_unit,
  u.total_quantity * p.price_per_unit AS line_amount
FROM billing.usage_summary u
JOIN billing.pricing_rules p
  ON u.metric_name = p.metric_name
  AND u.total_quantity BETWEEN
      p.tier_min AND p.tier_max;

Invoice Generation

At period end, a stored procedure snapshots the rated usage, applies final rules (committed use deduction, minimum charges), and produces a JSON invoice record ready for your billing system.

The snapshot uses Time Travel semantics: if a customer disputes an invoice, you can query exactly what the meter read at billing time.

CREATE OR REPLACE PROCEDURE billing.generate_invoice(
  p_tenant_id VARCHAR,
  p_billing_month DATE
)
RETURNS VARIANT
LANGUAGE SQL
AS
BEGIN
  LET invoice := (
    SELECT OBJECT_CONSTRUCT(
      'tenant_id',     :p_tenant_id,
      'billing_month', :p_billing_month,
      'generated_at',  CURRENT_TIMESTAMP(),
      'line_items',    ARRAY_AGG(
        OBJECT_CONSTRUCT(
          'metric',   metric_name,
          'quantity', total_quantity,
          'rate',     price_per_unit,
          'amount',   line_amount
        )
      ),
      'subtotal',      SUM(line_amount),
      'committed_use', 500.00,
      'overage',       GREATEST(
        SUM(line_amount) - 500.00, 0),
      'total_due',     GREATEST(
        SUM(line_amount), 500.00)
    )
    FROM billing.rated_usage
    WHERE tenant_id = :p_tenant_id
      AND billing_month = :p_billing_month
  );

  -- Store the invoice snapshot
  INSERT INTO billing.invoices
    (tenant_id, billing_month, invoice_data)
  VALUES
    (:p_tenant_id, :p_billing_month, :invoice);

  RETURN :invoice;
END;

-- Generate Acme's June invoice
CALL billing.generate_invoice('acme', '2026-06-01');
